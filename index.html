02 · Research

Choosing the right retrieval method, per document

Interlude · every dot is a real document

698 legal documents.

Non-disclosure agreements, commercial contracts, merger agreements and privacy policies from the LegalBench-RAG benchmark. This is where my MSc started.

Four sources, four shapes

They are not built alike.

CUAD 462, MAUD 134, ContractNLI 95, PrivacyQA 7. MAUD runs longest and is densest with cross-references; ContractNLI is the shortest.

Stratified by length

186 documents, every method run on each.

ContractNLI and PrivacyQA kept in full, CUAD and MAUD sampled across length quartiles with a fixed seed, checked against the full population.

Document-level wins

No method wins everywhere.

Each dot moves to the method with the highest F1 on that document. Dense wins most often, but BM25 leads on ContractNLI and RAPTOR still takes 36.

Indexing time per document

And the clever methods are expensive.

Always running RAPTOR costs 318.25 s per document. My random-forest router, choosing per document before any indexing, costs 3.70 s. Each dot is the same slice of time.

The result

One decision per document.

Highest F1 of every strategy on unseen documents (0.0753), about 86× less indexing than always-RAPTOR, ahead of RAPTOR in all five folds (p = 0.030).

01 / 06

Structure-aware routing for legal RAG

MSc dissertation · Brunel University London · supervised by Prof. Yongmin Li

  • Analysed the structure of 698 legal documents from four LegalBench-RAG datasets: ContractNLI, CUAD, MAUD and PrivacyQA.
  • Compared five retrieval methods (BM25, dense, hybrid, an adapted RAPTOR and a lightweight GraphRAG) over 1,957 queries, scored with precision, recall and F1 against the official answer locations.
  • Found no single method best everywhere, and that the advanced methods cost far more to index. Trained a random-forest router that picks a method per document from five cheap structural features, before any indexing.
  • On unseen documents the router had the highest F1 of every strategy, with about 86× less indexing time than always running RAPTOR. It beat RAPTOR in all five cross-validation folds (permutation test p = 0.030).
